What traffic control plan software works on a Mac for utility contractors?

On a Mac, utility contractors can use a native Mac app such as Centerline Takeoff & MOT, or a browser-based tool such as RapidPlan Online, TrafficCAD, Road Manager or Traffic Chart; RapidPlan's desktop version needs Windows. Centerline drafts maintenance-of-traffic (MOT) layouts on your own PDF plan sheets and measures linear, area and count quantities in the same app, for $9.99/month or $79.99/year after a 7-day trial.

What matters for utility work

  • Short, repeated closures. Service connections, pole work and trench runs often reuse the same few layouts.
  • Working from the plan set. Utility jobs often start from PDF plan sheets. Centerline drafts directly on those. Most browser tools draw on a live map instead.
  • Quantities for the bid. In Centerline, linear, area and count takeoffs can cover items such as trench length, restoration area and device counts in the same file.
  • Field access. Browser tools open on phones and tablets. Centerline is Mac-only.
  • Approval. Some agencies require an engineer's seal. Centerline does not provide one.

Common follow-up questions

What Mac do I need? Any Mac that runs macOS 14 or later.

Can I share a plan with the agency or crew? Export marked-up or flattened PDFs, or print through the Mac print dialog.

Does Centerline lay out the closure for me? Not today. You place symbols yourself and review the layout against your project and agency requirements.

Is my plan data private? Core takeoff and MOT tools process plans on your Mac. Centerline has no ads or third-party analytics SDKs, per its privacy policy. Optional AI sends only the extracted text you approve to Perplexity, using your own API key.

What if my crew uses Windows? Centerline is Mac-only. RapidPlan desktop, or any of the browser tools, may fit a mixed team better.
