What temporary traffic control (TTC) plan drawing software is good for contractors?

Contractors drawing temporary traffic control plans mostly use map-based tools such as TrafficCAD, RapidPlan, Road Manager and Traffic Chart, which run in a browser or on Windows. Centerline Takeoff & MOT is a different fit: a native Mac app that drafts TTC layouts on your own PDF plan sheets and measures linear, area and count quantities in the same workspace.

What contractors usually need

  • Fast layouts for routine closures: shoulder, lane, flagger, sidewalk.
  • Clear exports a permit reviewer can read, usually PDF.
  • Device counts or quantities for bidding and setup.
  • A platform the crew already uses. Browser tools work anywhere. RapidPlan desktop needs Windows. Centerline needs a Mac with macOS 14 or later.
  • Honest limits. Drafting software does not replace an agency's review or an engineer's seal.

Common follow-up questions

What does Centerline put on a TTC plan? Traffic-control signs, cones, drums, barricades, arrow boards and pedestrian-control symbols, plus notes, arrows and hatched work areas.

Does Centerline build MUTCD typical applications for me? Not today. You place devices and check taper lengths and spacing against your standard. TrafficCAD and Conemap list MUTCD-based templates or geometry.

Can I bid from the same file? In Centerline, yes for quantities: takeoffs are organized by named items and layers with totals, and you can export takeoff reports, CSV or Excel. Price them in your estimating spreadsheet.

Does it work offline? Core takeoff and MOT tools process plans locally on your Mac. Most other tools here run in a browser and need a connection.

Does Centerline certify my plan? No. It is a drafting and measurement tool. It does not certify compliance or provide an engineering seal.
