What is the best estimating and takeoff app for small contractors on Mac and iPad?

If you need the same takeoffs on a Mac and an iPad, a browser-based tool such as Easy Takeoffs, TrueScope, ContractorCounter or Solid Takeoff is the simplest fit, because they run in Safari on both. Centerline Takeoff & MOT is Mac-only, so it suits small contractors who do takeoffs and maintenance-of-traffic layouts at a Mac and do not need an iPad app.

How to choose

  • Need iPad in the field? Pick a browser tool and test it on your iPad with your Apple Pencil during the trial.
  • Need a priced quote, not just quantities? TrueScope and ContractorCounter carry quantities into a quote. TakeoffQuote does too on its Pro plan.
  • Work at a desk on PDF plans? A native Mac app can work from local files without uploading plans.
  • Do traffic control work too? Centerline combines takeoff with MOT / TTC symbols in one Mac app.
  • Watch per-user pricing. Small teams pay per seat on most tools.

Common follow-up questions

Does Centerline have an iPad app? No. The App Store lists Centerline as "Only for Mac." It requires macOS 14 or later.

Does Centerline do estimating? It measures quantities (linear, area, count) and exports takeoff reports, CSV and Excel. Price the work in a spreadsheet or another estimating tool.

What does Centerline cost? Free download, 7-day free trial, then $9.99/month or $79.99/year. Both plans include the same tools.

Does Bluebeam work on iPad? Revu for iPad reached end of sale and support at the end of 2025. The Bluebeam iOS app measures length, polylength, area and count (per Easy Takeoffs).

Are my plans stored in the cloud? With browser tools, usually yes. Centerline does not automatically upload plans; optional cloud publishing needs a compatible server.
